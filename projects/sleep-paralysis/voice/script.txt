A man once told a court that a woman came into his bedroom at night, sat on his chest, and grabbed his throat. He could not move. He could not scream. She was hanged for it.
What he described was almost certainly sleep paralysis.
So what is actually happening? Every night, when you dream, your brain switches off your muscles so you don't act your dreams out. Sometimes your mind wakes up before that switch turns back on. You are awake, but your body is still locked.
And part of your brain is still dreaming. That is where the figure comes from. Your brain senses danger, and the dream gives it a shape, right there in your real room.
The weight on your chest? In dream sleep your breathing is shallow, and you can't force a deep breath. Your brain reads that as something pressing down on you.
It usually lasts a few seconds to a couple of minutes. About one in twelve people will go through it at least once. Too little sleep, stress, and sleeping on your back make it more likely.
It feels terrifying. But it cannot hurt you. Follow for more.
