# Reference video breakdown + English script

Reference: 480x854 (9:16), 30 fps, 116.5 s, Hindi voiceover, English captions.
About 45 scenes, average 2.5 s each. Nothing on screen is ever still.

## 1. How it is built

### Assets
- Every object is a **photoreal still image cut out on transparency** (kadhai, thali, phone, hand, lungs, kidney, pots, pencil, stamp, bag, tiles). The look is consistent with AI-generated product-style images; a few shots (boiling tomato, steam, the tumbling kadhai, the glomerulus) move in a way that suggests short image-to-video clips or real 3D rather than a flat cutout.
- Backgrounds are **flat textured colour walls**, and the colour is a code for the chapter:
  - green wall + tile counter = home kitchen
  - purple bokeh = social media / the rumour
  - mustard yellow = the evidence desk (facts, documents)
  - orange/red = factory
  - maroon = inside the body
  - blue = fridge / storage
  - sunset red-brown = scrapyard and foundry
- Documents are **real screenshots** (IARC monograph list, WHO/IARC letterhead, a journal abstract, an FDA notice) printed onto paper-textured cards.

### Making flat images feel 3D (2.5D)
- Each cutout is a separate layer at a different distance from a virtual camera. The camera always pushes in or drifts, so layers slide against each other (parallax).
- Every object has a **soft contact shadow** that is its own layer; when an object lifts or drops, the shadow shrinks/grows and moves apart from it.
- **Depth of field**: foreground and background layers are blurred, the subject is sharp. Rack focus is used as a transition (factory goes soft, lungs come up sharp).
- Cards, tiles and phones are tilted in 3D space (perspective skew) and rotate as they land, with motion blur on.
- A **light sweep / glint** crosses metal surfaces so they read as shiny and solid.
- True 3D only where needed: the kadhai tumbling out of the window shows its underside, and the Pb tile falls with real perspective and thickness.

### Motion vocabulary
- Pop-in with overshoot (scale 0 -> 110% -> 100%).
- Slide in from off-frame with heavy motion blur, small settle rotation.
- Drop from top + bounce + dust puff (Pb tile).
- Duplication: one message card becomes three; one phone becomes a wall of phones.
- Write-on strokes: red pencil draws an arrow and handwrites "CANCER" with an underline; red crosses; green tick; green highlighter. The pencil/pen cutout follows the stroke tip.
- Stamp slam: stamp arrives blurred, hits, lifts, leaves the "NO PROOF" ink.
- Magnifying glass sweeps across the kadhai, then parks.
- Hanging tags swing on their string; green "SAFE" tags flip to red "?".
- Particles: silver balls rise out of the food, fly into a 10x10 grid, 99 drop away and one glows.
- Steam, smoke and furnace sparks as soft looping overlays.

### Hands
Hands are separate cutout layers entering from the bottom or side edge, always already holding something (roti, phone, pot, glass box). They are animated only with position, a few degrees of rotation and motion blur, so they never need finger animation. They are used to make a point feel like a human action: eating, scrolling, putting a pot away.

### Captions
- Fixed position, top-left, two lines, all caps, tall condensed bold face (Anton / Bebas Neue style).
- Line 1 small and white; line 2 large with a vertical gradient and glow, coloured to match the scene (pink on purple, blue on green, yellow on factory, teal on maroon, red for danger words).
- Line 1 appears on the first word, line 2 on the punch word. Old caption fades out as the new one fades in.
- They are 2-5 word summaries, not a transcript.

### Global finish
One overlay sits on top of the whole video and glues it together: paper/film texture with a vertical crease at about 62% width, a column of small punch-hole dots down the left edge, vignette, and light grain.

## 4. English voiceover script

About 290 words, about 1:55 at a brisk pace. Same facts and order as the original, reworded.

**0:00** Does cooking in aluminium pots really give you cancer? Or is it just a rumour?

**0:06** You've heard it on podcasts, in articles, in videos: aluminium cookware causes cancer, so throw it out of your kitchen today.

**0:16** But the truth isn't that simple. So far, research has found no solid proof that aluminium pots cause cancer.

**0:25** Here's the surprising part. Aluminium really is on the WHO cancer agency's list. But not the pot. The factory that makes aluminium, where workers breathed furnace fumes for years and developed lung and bladder cancer.

**0:40** Your kadhai has no furnace and no fumes.

**0:43** And of all the aluminium you eat in a day, less than one percent ever reaches your blood. The rest leaves your body, and whatever does get in, your kidneys filter out. In people with weak kidneys, though, it can build up.

**0:56** But that doesn't mean every aluminium pot is safe. First: sour and salty food eats away the thin protective skin on the metal, and aluminium starts seeping into your food. So don't cook or store things like tomato or tamarind in an uncoated pot.

**1:12** Second, and this is the bigger one: lead. Many cheap pots are cast from melted scrap, old car parts and old electronics, and the lead in that scrap stays in the pot.

**1:23** A 2025 study tested pots from 25 countries, and India was among those with the most lead. The US FDA has even warned about certain Indian-made aluminium kadhais that leach lead into food, and told people to throw them away.

**1:38** So next time someone says aluminium pots cause cancer, remember: there's no solid proof. But if there's a cheap, scrap-cast pot in your kitchen, that's where the real danger may be. And it isn't aluminium. It's lead.

**1:52** What do you cook in at home? Tell us in the comments.

Note: the study and FDA claims are carried over from the original narration and were not independently checked here. Check the sources before publishing.
